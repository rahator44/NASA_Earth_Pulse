01 // SCIENTIFIC PHILOSOPHY & SCOPE DECISION SUPPORT
“We do not predict disasters. We reveal, visualize, quantify, and explain observed changes to Earth's surface using NISAR and complementary Earth-observation data.”

The NISAR Surface Change Explorer is an evidence-first Earth-observation decision-support workspace. It operates strictly on physical radiometric backscatter differences, interferometric phase correlations, and cross-sensor validation.

This application does not issue evacuation instructions or provide tactical crisis command directives.

03. NISAR PRODUCT ROLES & PHYSICAL SPECIFICATIONS

PHYSICAL MODES
Crucial Physical Rule: NISAR products are NOT interchangeable. Each product encodes fundamentally different wave interactions (radiometric backscatter power, interferometric phase fringes, or sub-pixel speckle correlation offsets).
GCOV LEVEL 2
Geocoded Radiometric Backscatter

Terrain-corrected normalized radar cross-section (gamma-nought $\gamma^0$) in dual polarization (HH, HV). Measures surface roughness, dielectric permittivity, and volumetric structure.

Primary Modules: Flood / Wetland Inundation • Vegetation Disturbance
GUNW LEVEL 2
Geocoded Unwrapped Interferogram

Relative line-of-sight (LOS) spatial displacement and interferometric phase coherence derived from repeat-pass single-look complex (SLC) radar returns.

Primary Modules: Ground Deformation • Geodetic Bedrock Baselines
GOFF LEVEL 2
Geocoded Pixel Offsets

Along-track and slant-range cross-correlation offsets tracking fast movement where radar phase coherence has decorrelated.

Primary Modules: Glacier Flow Dynamics • Ice Shelf Fracture Tracking

04. COVERAGE DISCOVERY & NINE-POINT PAIR COMPATIBILITY

ASF DAAC ENGINE

Coverage queries query the Alaska Satellite Facility (ASF DAAC) metadata catalog via asf_search. Acquisitions are filtered to the post-launch public calibration window (2026-06-17 onward). Products are evaluated separately for PROVISIONAL (preferred calibrated public release) and BETA maturity states without silent mixing.

GCOV Pair Selection Policy

The primary automated policy is nearest_previous: searching for the nearest earlier compatible acquisition (nominally 12-day or 24-day exact repeat orbital interval). A fixed reference policy (fixed_reference) is available when evaluating seasonal hydrologic baselines.

The Nine-Point Scientific Compatibility Engine:

Two acquisitions are marked COMPATIBLE only when all 9 physical criteria hold:

1 Same Track (Relative Orbit)
2 Same Frame Number
3 Same Orbit Direction (Asc/Desc)
4 Compatible Acquisition Mode
5 Compatible Polarizations (HH/HV)
6 Compatible Radar Frequency
7 Compatible Bandwidth (MHz)
8 Same Product Maturity State
9 Compatible CRID / Processor Version
GUNW / GOFF Distinct Handling: Neither GUNW interferograms nor GOFF velocity products are paired using GCOV two-date backscatter logic. GUNW and GOFF granules encode mission-processed interferometric baselines and correlation pairs directly within their product packaging.

05. THREE-BRANCH SAR PROCESSING ARCHITECTURE

PHYSICAL PIPELINES
Physical Ordering Rule: Multi-look speckle filtering is mathematically evaluated in LINEAR POWER space prior to decibel log-transform ($\text{dB} = 10 \log_{10} \gamma^0$). Linear speckle averaging must never be computed after non-linear logarithmic transformation.
Branch A: GCOV Radiometric Difference Pipeline DESIGNED • NEXT MILESTONE
Crop AOI → Valid Pixel Mask (≥ 85%) → Linear Power Lee Speckle Filter → Convert Power to dB → Align Compatible Pair → Compute Difference (Δγ°) → Adaptive Bimodal Cutoff → Vector Delineation
Branch B: GUNW Phase & Relative LOS Pipeline NOT YET IMPLEMENTED
Crop AOI → InSAR Coherence Mask (≥ 0.4) → Select Stable Bedrock Reference Area → Compute Relative LOS Residual → Flag Non-Trivial Deformation Footprints

06. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC DETECTION LOGIC & SCIENTIFIC TERMINOLOGY

PHYSICAL SIGNATURES

HYDROLOGY: FLOOD & WETLAND CHANGE

GCOV DUAL-POL

Evaluates two complementary physical radar signatures:

  • Signature A (Specular Attenuation): Smooth open water acts as a specular reflector, scattering incident radar pulses away from the sensor. Backscatter drops significantly (Δγ° ≤ -3.5 dB).
  • Signature B (Double-Bounce Scattering): Inundated tree trunks and emergent reed vegetation produce double-bounce dihedral reflections, increasing backscatter. Evaluated only where vegetation land-cover classes exist.
Approved terminology: “Radar-observed inundation / wetland change.” Permanent water and steep slope masks are applied to prevent false detections.

BIOSPHERE: VEGETATION DISTURBANCE

GCOV CROSS-POL

Measures the destruction or desiccation of forest canopies and shrublands. Cross-polarized (HV) radar backscatter is highly sensitive to volume scattering from branches and leaves.

  • Radar-Only Detection: Severe canopy defoliation or crown consumption produces a sharp loss in HV volume scattering.
  • Mandatory Phrasing: Standalone radar observations are labeled “Radar-detected vegetation disturbance.”
  • Fire Attribution: The label “Vegetation disturbance consistent with fire” is assigned ONLY when corroborated by independent NASA FIRMS thermal hotspots. Never use “Radar-detected fire.”

CRYOSPHERE: GLACIER DYNAMICS

GOFF LEVEL 2

Ingests NISAR GOFF sub-pixel speckle tracking products to determine ice velocity magnitude and azimuth direction. Does not assume global GOFF availability; operates strictly over monitored glacier zones.

GEOHAZARDS: RELATIVE LOS DEFORMATION

GUNW InSAR

Interferometric phase residuals are evaluated against stable bedrock reference networks. Crucial scientific caveat: Line-of-sight (LOS) displacement ≠ vertical subsidence. Single-track InSAR measures only 1D projection along radar sightlines.

07. FIVE PHYSICAL OBSERVATION STATES & UNKNOWN HANDLING

CANONICAL DEFINITIONS
NON-ALARM PRINCIPLE: Observation States represent empirical radar evidence. They are deliberately rendered using restrained scientific tokens (cool gray, royal blue, cyan, violet, slate) rather than emergency siren colors (green, yellow, red).
NO CHANGE

Permitted ONLY when valid repeat coverage exists, quality gates pass, and change magnitude remains below domain threshold criteria.

CHANGE DETECTED

Significant physical surface change observed matching an established radar backscatter or phase signature.

STRONG CHANGE

High-magnitude divergence and large contiguous spatial extent exceeding strong-change criteria.

UNKNOWN CHANGE

High-confidence physical change verified, but conflicting or insufficient independent data prevents specific physical attribution.

INSUFFICIENT DATA

Valid pixel fraction below quality gate (< 85%) or missing compatible repeat pass. Insufficient Data ≠ No Change.

Public Wording for Unknown Change:

“Unusual radar change detected. Cause not identified.”

Surface changes from agricultural harvesting, soil moisture variations, freeze/thaw transitions, and defoliation produce radar divergence. UNKNOWN CHANGE is preserved honestly rather than forcing an inaccurate hazard classification.

08. MULTI-CANDIDATE INTERPRETATION & FIRMS CORROBORATION

EVIDENCE RESOLUTION

A single radar backscatter decrease may support competing physical hypotheses (e.g. smooth water inundation vs complete defoliation). The system stores candidate records containing:

Physical Evidence
Independent Evidence
Contradicting Factors
Support Status
Explainability rule IDs are application rules, not NASA hazard codes.

IDs such as GCOV_BACKSCATTER_DECREASE_01 record the exact deterministic rule used to turn a stored measurement into cautious public wording. They do not claim that NISAR itself publishes a flood, fire, danger, or risk code.

Public Mode shows the measurement, why the rule can support an interpretation, alternative explanations, and the limit of the conclusion. Scientist Mode also exposes the rule ID, product metadata, thresholds, and provenance.

NASA FIRMS Corroboration Engine Defaults:
  • Temporal Coincidence Window: Between reference and secondary pass dates ± 24 hours.
  • Spatial Proximity Buffer: Within detected change boundary or 1,000 m geographic buffer.
  • Caveat: FIRMS thermal detections indicate active combustion at scan time, not a complete structural burn scar perimeter.

09. VALIDATION FRAMEWORK & BENCHMARK METRICS

NO GENERIC CONFIDENCE
Calibration Events

Historical surface change events used to tune backscatter threshold cutoffs, speckle filter kernel sizes, and spatial component filters.

Independent Validation Events

Unseen external events with ground-truth water gauge, GNSS, or post-event aerial orthophotos used solely to compute benchmark metrics.

Scientific Integrity Mandates:

If calibration and evaluation occur on the same observation, the system enforces disclosure: “Calibration and evaluation used the same event; reported performance is not independent validation.”

The application never presents synthetic “confidence percentages.” It reports actual mathematical metrics: Intersection over Union (IoU), Precision, Recall, F1 score, and Displacement RMSE.

10. DETERMINISTIC REVIEW PRIORITY FORMULATION

REVIEW ORDERING
OPERATIONAL TRIAGE FORMULA ReviewScore = 0.50 × O + 0.35 × E + 0.15 × F
DETERMINISTIC WEIGHTS
O: Observation (50%)

Strong Change = 1.00 • Unknown Change = 0.65 • Change Detected = 0.50. No Change and Insufficient Data are never scored.

E: Exposure (35%)

0.60 × Population + 0.40 × Infrastructure. Normalized dataset-relatively against maximums in the active comparison set.

F: Freshness (15%)

Calculated relative to the newest observation in the dataset using piecewise linear decay (0d: 1.0, 7d: 0.8, 14d: 0.6, 30d: 0.3, >60d: 0.1).

Dense-City Ranking Caveat:

“A smaller detected change in a densely populated area can rank above a larger change in a sparsely populated area because Review Priority includes exposure.”

Review Priority reflects human review and verification ordering, NOT physical magnitude or hazard severity.

13. DOCUMENTED SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS

BOUNDS
• Current Event Records Are Synthetic Fixtures: Visual imagery SVGs, histograms, and exposure metrics demonstrate the UI workflow rather than active NISAR radiometry.
• Metadata vs Raster Distinction: Archive discovery through ASF DAAC is live, but numeric on-demand SAR raster differencing is not yet running in this demo step.
• Precomputed Threshold Previews: Image Lab threshold sliders interpolate between discrete stored mask states and do not re-run SAR calibration in the browser.
• Dataset-Relative Normalization: Review Priority scores are relative to the active comparison cohort and adjust if the review set changes.
• Coverage Availability ≠ Change: A high density of compatible NISAR passes over an AOI does not imply surface disturbance has occurred.
• Missing Analysis ≠ No Change: Unanalyzed footprints cannot be assumed stable; lack of observation data represents an absence of analysis, not confirmed stability.
